## 💡 Vision & Purpose
Going into the summer after my freshman year, I knew I wanted hands-on experience with preal people and real customers. Building full-stack projects in a controlled environment is great, but working as a Summer IT Intern for the **College of Sciences (COS IT) at NC State** gave me a look at the other side of technology: keeping infrastructure running and helping real people solve technical roadblocks.

---

## 🛠️ What I Actually Did Day-to-Day

Rather than working on a single isolated task, my role required context-switching between technical execution and clear communication:

* **Hardware Deployment & Provisioning:** Prepared, imaged, and deployed workstations and laptops for faculty, staff, and research labs across campus.
* **Troubleshooting & Support:** Handled helpdesk tickets involving operating system configuration, network connectivity, permission issues, and software compatibility.
* **System Diagnostics:** Diagnosed hardware failures and managed device inventory.
* **Client Communication:** Translated technical jargon into plain language when assisting faculty and department staff with active technical issues.

---

## 🧠 Key Takeaways & Lessons Learned

Working in IT support changed how I think about tech-related roles in geenral.

1. **Communication is Key:** Technical skill is only half the battle. Learning how to ask the right questions and patiently guide someone through a fix. When unaware of how to solve the issue yourself, talking to your coworkers is the best thing to do. Being able to talk comfortmably with them is a very thing and keeps the good mood, even when the work is difficult.
2. **Don't Be Scared to Ask Questions:** Starting out in a large university environment everything felt overwhelming, and it wasa tempting to try and figure everything out alone to look capable. I quickly learned that asking questions isn't a sign of weakness. It's how you avoid wasting time and learn proper workflows from people who have been doing it for years.
3. **Mistakes Happen:** In IT and tech in general, you're bound to misconfigure something or hit an unexpected error at some point. The important lesson wasn't avoiding mistakes entirely, but taking ownership, staying calm, and focusing on how to fix the issue quickly while documenting what happened so it doesn't happen twice.

---

## 🚀 Moving Forward

This internship gave me a grounded perspective on how professional environments are. Combining this practical IT experience with my full-stack projects (like ScreenScore and Bookshelf) gives me a much clearer picture of how things look like in the real world, preparing me for my next experience.
